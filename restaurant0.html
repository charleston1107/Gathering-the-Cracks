回忆使美好更美好

Liang Zheng, the restaurant owner, grew up in Bacheng Old Street and recalls a childhood steeped in vibrant communal life and intimate ties to place. In his telling, the old street of the 1980s was filled with everyday activity anchored by the canal: residents lived literally “by the water”

Redevelopment and the Loss of Living Heritage

What was once organic and specific has been made generic: “Now if you go to Bacheng Old Street, or just go anywhere in Suzhou, Jiangsu, or Zhejiang, the old streets all feel almost the same”, he observes wryly. Crucially, he argues, the new old street lacks human warmth and everyday presence. “I don’t like the old street now… I just feel it has detached from people’s lives” – as if “its feet no longer touch the ground”

Preserving “Old Taste” and Local Agency

Through his eyes, we see a Bacheng caught between spectre and spectacle—the spectre of a rich communal past, and the spectacle of a staged heritage present. By running his modest restaurant, he has created a space to keep tradition alive. The shop has a straightforward menu, which lacks hyper-touristic makeovers

Not Paying for Stories

As he notes, his clientele still includes many locals who seek comfort in an honest bowl of noodles rather than a fabricated story. In this way, Zheng emerges as a custodian of intangible heritage, bridging past and present. He is at once witness and guardian, articulating the community’s sense of displacement while actively asserting the value of its memory and culture.
