The first time we came to Bacheng, the old house of red brick stood quietly by the river. When we returned, it had already collapsed into scattered bricks. The trees beside it, once leaning like silent companions, had fallen too.

If one pays attention, the traces of demolition are everywhere—fragments of red brick walls punctuating the streets, new concrete houses rise beside them, smooth and pale, attempting to overwrite what came before. Yet for a fleeting moment, the old and the new stand side by side, like two epochs accidentally inhabiting the same breath.
